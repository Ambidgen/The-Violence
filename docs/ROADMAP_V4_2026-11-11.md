### 2.3 What the numbers say

Latest matrix (patrol run, 8 seeds × 4 policies, from `docs/EA_TASKS.md`):

| Policy | What it does | Officers killed per run | Weeks under 12 fit | Lowest balance | Run score |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **minimal** | No hiring, courses, contracts or programs; declines every offer; one call a week; never answers the radio | **7.3** | **6** | **+$38k** | +5 |
| military-first | Military contracts and kit, hire to 16, three calls a week | 12 | 17 | −$34k | +24 |
| accept-all | Accepts everything, hire to 18, three calls a week | 16 | 16 | −$64k | +9 |
| cautious | Hire, train, restraint contracts, the gravest two calls | 16 | 16 | −$55k | **−122** |

On the armour matrix, which reports more columns, `minimal` also ended with $232k unspent, lost the fewest allies (1.1 against 2.5 to 2.8) and kept the office in 8 of 8 runs.

Why it happens, from the code:

- **Answering a call is a bad bet.** The run score pays +10 for a success and +3 for an arrest, and charges −25 for an officer killed. A crew-house raid kills 0.44 officers on average, which is −11 before the replacement bill.
- **Leaving a call is nearly free.** It costs −3 on the score, one or two points of ward trust, a small poll move, and sometimes a Ledger story. Nothing in the city gets worse.
- **The gate does not test for it.** The 28 Oct roadmap's Gate 1 said no policy may dominate and `minimal` must not end with an unspent six-figure account. That line never reached `balance_bands.json`: none of its 13 bands tests for a dominant policy.
