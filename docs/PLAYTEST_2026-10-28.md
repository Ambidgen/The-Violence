# Playtest — The Violence, current 1.3 candidate

**Date:** 2026-10-28 · **Source:** `41ccd8a` · **Displayed/runtime version:** 1.2.1

## Verdict

The core loop is there: the city board, intelligence-led planning, hands-off operation, consequential debrief and persistent campaign all connect. The first-operation guide and the explicit warning that readiness is **not** a win probability are good foundations. I would not release this build yet: an unprovoked crowd escalation and a duplicate paid contract are reproducible, and the campaign and release gates need another pass.

## What I actually exercised

- Used the **official Godot 4.7.2 Web runtime** in headless Chromium with software WebGL at 1280×720. Mouse-driven visual checks reached the title, settings, new run, inheritance, board, briefing and an in-progress operation. These were automated interactions, not a human usability study.
- Separately drove the actual UI button signals through a new run with seed **410255**: chose plate-carrier inheritance and an SMG contract; opened a barricade briefing with seven suggested officers; visited Force and Orders; ran the unchanged suggestion; inspected the debrief; sought the replay; loaded the saved campaign; and ended week 1. For speed the simulation was stepped directly and the normal operation-finished method was called rather than waiting through real-time animation. The result was **partial success, 65 points**, no officer or civilian casualties, and $805 overtime. Opening the briefing did **not** change the account. The save reloaded with the call marked done, and the campaign advanced to week 2. See the [walkthrough log](../tests/results/playtest-2026-10-28/logs/ui-walkthrough.log).
- Ran the existing crowd probe for four matched seeds with two officers, morale 60 and **no attack**, using the normal roster's trauma floor and a floor of 14. Ran the existing `op_test.gd` for four weeks, seed **1001**, `--chief=sensible --rogue`, and compared the older 35-case determinism fixture.

**Important environment limits:** This was a temporary Web test pack, not the game's standard export. The game GDScript was unchanged; the pack was assembled manually, PNG/SVG textures and the font were repackaged, sound effects were omitted, and visual captures were capped at 12 fps. The harnesses were adapted only from `SceneTree` startup to a `Node` scene and given fixed arguments so they could run in the Web template. Their `quit()` calls were replaced with a completion marker: `PROBE_FINISHED errors=0` means no browser/engine errors, **not** that a fixture comparison passed; use the printed PASS/FAIL. The intro was disabled. Consequently this does **not** verify native asset imports, audio, cutscenes, real-time performance, Windows, or a shipping executable. The visibility dots appeared as missing-glyph boxes in this temporary pack; verify that separately in a normal native export before treating it as a product defect. All logs and screenshots are under [`tests/results/playtest-2026-10-28/`](../tests/results/playtest-2026-10-28/).

## Evidence that drives the priorities

### Crowd behavior

| Trauma floor | Quiet-event seeds | Civilian deaths reported | Police rounds hitting civilians | Police shots | Misjudgments |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 (normal roster; not forced to zero) | 5000, 5017, 5034, 5051 | 3 | 3 | 9 | 1 |
| 14 | Same four seeds | 25 | 43 | 92 | 23 |

All eight events reported `attack=false`. The incident's `civ_hit_police` is a **round-hit count**, not a count of distinct people; `civ_killed` is a person count and does not by itself attribute each death. This is a small controlled sample, not a campaign-wide casualty rate. Both the control's three deaths and the high-trauma cascade warrant attention. [Control log](../tests/results/playtest-2026-10-28/logs/crowd-control.log) · [Trauma-14 log](../tests/results/playtest-2026-10-28/logs/crowd-trauma-14.log).

### Duplicate procurement

With a fresh legacy and campaign **seed 1**, the initial drafts contain `plates` in both inheritance and procurement. Choose **Plate carriers on day one**, then purchase **Plate carriers**: the balance falls from **$25,000 to $19,000**, the armoury is unchanged, and `contracts` becomes `["plates", "plates"]`. The $6,000 contract adds no new item. [Probe log](../tests/results/playtest-2026-10-28/logs/procurement-duplicate.log).

This is also visible in a separate UI run: the [board after choosing the plate inheritance](../tests/results/playtest-2026-10-28/screens/board-after-inheritance.jpg) leads to [a procurement screen still offering plates for $6,000](../tests/results/playtest-2026-10-28/screens/procurement-after-plates.jpg). The screenshots show the offer; the seeded probe verifies the charge. The draft is dealt before the inheritance is chosen, and `pick()` does not revalidate it against current stock (`scripts/campaign/rogue.gd`).

### Early campaign and regression signals

The four-week automated run completed **eight operations**, with **three officer deaths**. Its pre-end-of-week snapshots went from 20 fit and $23,890 in week 1 to **14 fit and $9,534 in week 4**, with nine total findings. This chief did not hire or train. More importantly, the harness issues suggested kit automatically and resolves the roguelike desk **after** operations; the current UI uses existing kit and requires inheritance first. These numbers are a warning to investigate, not a prediction of a human campaign. [Campaign log](../tests/results/playtest-2026-10-28/logs/campaign-four-weeks.log).

The Web run of the older determinism fixture reported **22 changed of 35 cases**: 20 tactical and two campaign cases, including changed outcomes. The README already warns that the fixture predates the latest simulation changes; platform differences may also matter. The newer eight-case range-only fixture matched **six** hashes; the motel and large-team hashes differed even though their displayed winners, times and shot counts matched. These comparisons do **not** identify the cause or establish that native builds fail identically. Review on the supported native engines before updating a fixture. [Older fixture log](../tests/results/playtest-2026-10-28/logs/determinism-web.log) · [Range fixture log](../tests/results/playtest-2026-10-28/logs/range-determinism-web.log).

## Five recommendations, in order

### 1. Stop the crowd-escalation cascade — release blocker

Fix the judgment/stress feedback in the simulation, not just the suggested-plan preference for lower-trauma officers. Make recovery after a mistaken civilian engagement bounded, and check that panic cannot repeatedly compound the mistake. Extend `tests/event_probe.gd` to record **distinct** civilians struck, not just rounds, and test quiet and actual-attack events at several trauma/morale levels. A proposed gate is no more than one police-struck person in each of at least 20 quiet high-trauma seeded events, while legitimate threats still get a response; agree the acceptable threshold with the designer. Retest the normal-trauma control too.

### 2. Revalidate outstanding procurement choices — high priority

When inheritance or another offer changes stock, replace, disable or clearly label any now-redundant pending contract. Recheck immediately before payment as well, so a stale draft cannot charge for nothing or append duplicate contracts. Add a regression for seed 1 and for a contract invalidated by an intervening offer: balance, stock and contract IDs must all remain consistent. Preserve the option to pass or select another useful card.

### 3. Make the first week readable and self-explanatory — high priority

At 1280×720, the right-hand board and briefing text is very small; the first call appears below several paragraphs, and the reason its plan button is disabled is available as a tooltip. The board's city map is helpful, but opening a desk draft replaces it with the unrelated **Test Range 00** layout on the left. Keep a relevant city/desk context there or deliberately hide the stale map; make the next required action and disabled-state reason visible without hover; offer a persistent UI/text scale. Check font fallback and the debrief's singular/plural wording as part of the pass. Validate with new players at laptop and 1600×900 sizes, including the [first briefing](../tests/results/playtest-2026-10-28/screens/first-briefing.jpg), rather than relying on the scripted walkthrough alone.

### 4. Balance the real campaign loop with representative policies

First make the long-run harness match the UI: choose inheritance before the first operation, use current equipment unless the simulated chief explicitly buys kit, and model hiring/training/desk choices as named policies. Then run multiple matched seeds through 40 weeks, comparing at least a cautious hiring/training policy and a minimal-intervention policy. Record lowest fit roster, unhandled calls, casualties, account/deficit weeks, allies and election outcomes. Use those results to tune recovery, hiring and the money curve, and expose a next-week staffing/budget warning on the board. Do not tune from the single four-week sample above.

### 5. Turn the candidate into a verified release artifact

Activate the proposed CI workflow and run the quick, tactical and campaign gates on the supported native engines. Investigate both fixture comparisons on native builds and account for platform-specific numeric differences before deliberately rebaselining; do not use `--write` to hide unexplained changes. Decide and synchronize the release version (`scripts/version.gd`, `project.godot`, and export metadata), rebuild from this tree, and test the actual Windows build through boot, a week, save/reload, replay, resize/alt-tab and quit. Provide signing or a clear unsigned-build warning, checksums, a project licence and third-party notices; confirm the cinematic tooling's licence. Decide whether to wire or exclude the currently unreachable shorts.

One packaging-tool caveat: `tools/inspect_pck.py` accepts pack format 2 only. It rejected a **separate** Godot 4.7.2-generated Web pack with format 4; a native export of this game was not built here. Update or replace that verifier for the chosen export engine before relying on it as a release gate.

To reproduce the duplicate without a UI, use an isolated `user://` and run a `SceneTree` script containing:

```gdscript
extends SceneTree
const Campaign = preload("res://scripts/campaign/campaign.gd")

func _init() -> void:
    var c = Campaign.new()
    c.start(1, true, "user://fresh_legacy_for_probe.json")
    print(c.run.drafts)                  # both drafts contain "plates"
    c.run.pick(c, 0, "plates")           # inheritance; remaining draft is procurement
    var before: int = c.dept.balance
    var stock: Array = c.run.armory.duplicate()
    c.run.pick(c, 0, "plates")           # buys the already inherited item
    print("charged=", before - c.dept.balance,
        " unchanged_stock=", stock == c.run.armory, " contracts=", c.run.contracts)
    quit()
```
